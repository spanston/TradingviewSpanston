# Wyckoff Operating Model

This reference preserves the practical doctrine for macro Wyckoff reads. Load it when a user asks for detailed Wyckoff reasoning, phase labels, event definitions, or a full trading plan.

## Core Question

Wyckoff analysis asks: who controls the market right now, buyers or sellers, and where is the path of least resistance?

It is not a mechanical pattern system. It is an evidence framework for reading price, volume, market structure, liquidity, and context.

## Mindset

- Read the market by its own action.
- Context comes before entries.
- Labels are secondary to behavior.
- Everything is conditional until the market confirms imbalance.
- Recent meaningful evidence usually matters more than older evidence.
- Avoid forcing structures.

## Market Cycle

### Accumulation

Stronger participants absorb supply after a decline. Public emotion is often fearful. Expected result: markup.

Common evidence:

- Prior downtrend or bearish exhaustion
- Selling climax or stopping action
- Decreasing volatility and volume through the range
- Weak tests of the lows
- Spring or shakeout with quick recovery
- Later higher lows
- Strong breakout above resistance
- BUEC or pullback that holds

### Markup

Trend phase after accumulation. Evidence: higher highs, higher lows, strong bullish waves, controlled pullbacks, failed seller attempts to make lower lows.

### Distribution

Stronger participants sell inventory after an advance. Public emotion is often optimistic. Expected result: markdown.

Common evidence:

- Prior uptrend or bullish exhaustion
- Buying climax or stopping action
- Volatility and volume spikes
- Upthrusts above resistance
- Strong downward waves
- Lower highs late in the range
- Breakdown below support
- Weak rally back toward broken support

### Markdown

Trend phase after distribution. Evidence: lower lows, lower highs, strong bearish waves, weak rallies, failed buyer attempts to make higher highs.

### Reaccumulation and Redistribution

A range after an uptrend can be distribution or reaccumulation. A range after a downtrend can be accumulation or redistribution. The previous trend gives context, but the behavior inside the range determines the working scenario.

## Three Laws

### Supply and Demand

Price rises when demand is more aggressive than supply, or when supply withdraws. Price falls when supply is more aggressive than demand, or when demand withdraws. Support and resistance are areas where supply or demand previously appeared.

### Cause and Effect

Large movement usually needs preparation. The trading range builds the cause; the trend that follows is the effect. Longer, cleaner, more meaningful ranges can produce larger effects, but not every range is a useful campaign.

### Effort and Result

Effort is volume. Result is price progress.

Harmony:

- High volume produces strong directional movement.
- Low volume appears on corrective pullbacks.
- Breakout has wide spread, strong close, and supportive volume.

Divergence:

- High volume produces little progress.
- A level break quickly fails.
- New highs/lows travel less distance.
- Heavy rally cannot advance.
- Heavy decline cannot fall.

## Waves

Compare each wave by:

- Length: farther or shorter than prior waves?
- Speed: faster or slower?
- Volume: supported or fading?
- Result: structure break or failure?
- Reaction: what happens immediately after?

The comparison matters more than the absolute number.

## Trend Health

Use four tests:

- Projection: does each impulse make meaningful progress?
- Depth: are reactions shallow or deep?
- Speed: is price urgent or slowing?
- Volume: does effort support continuation or show absorption?

Healthy bullish trends show strong upward impulses and weaker reactions. Healthy bearish trends show strong downward impulses and weaker rallies.

## Trading Ranges

Ranges can represent accumulation, distribution, reaccumulation, redistribution, or random balance.

Treat boundaries as zones:

- In accumulation, the upper boundary is the Creek. A bullish breakout is Jump Across the Creek.
- In distribution, the lower boundary is the Ice. A bearish breakdown is Fall Through the Ice.

The best reads happen at range extremes because liquidity is concentrated there.

At range lows, ask:

- Can sellers continue lower?
- Is there absorption?
- Did price spring below support and recover?
- Did price fail to reach the lows, showing strength?

At range highs, ask:

- Can buyers continue higher?
- Is there absorption?
- Did price upthrust above resistance and reject?
- Did price fail to reach the highs, showing weakness?

## Phases

### Phase A: Stopping the Prior Trend

Accumulation events:

- PS: Preliminary Support
- SC: Selling Climax
- AR: Automatic Rally
- ST: Secondary Test

Distribution events:

- PSY: Preliminary Supply
- BC: Buying Climax
- AR: Automatic Reaction
- ST: Secondary Test

Purpose: identify that the prior trend has stopped and a range may be forming.

### Phase B: Building the Cause

Main construction phase. Price rotates, liquidity builds, weak hands are shaken, and control may appear mixed. Be patient and avoid final labels too early.

### Phase C: The Test

Accumulation tests:

- Spring: break below support and recovery
- Test of Spring: low-volume retest
- LPS: higher low confirming demand
- Terminal Shakeout: deeper violent spring followed by recovery

Distribution tests:

- UTAD: break above resistance and rejection after distribution
- UTAD test: retest after upthrust
- LPSY: lower high confirming supply

Phase C does not always hit the full extreme. Failure to reach an extreme can itself show control.

### Phase D: Trend Inside the Range

Accumulation evidence:

- Strong rally from Phase C
- SOS: Sign of Strength
- JAC: Jump Across the Creek
- Higher-low LPS reactions
- BUEC: Back Up to the Edge of the Creek

Distribution evidence:

- Strong decline from Phase C
- SOW: Sign of Weakness
- Fall through Ice
- LPSY lower highs
- Weak tests after breakdown

### Phase E: Trend Out of the Range

Bullish Phase E should make higher highs and higher lows. Bearish Phase E should make lower lows and lower highs. The job shifts from identifying the range to managing the trend.

## Event Definitions

- Preliminary Support: early buying during a decline, usually not enough to stop trend alone.
- Preliminary Supply: early selling during an advance, usually not enough to stop trend alone.
- Selling Climax: panic decline where heavy selling meets strong buying.
- Buying Climax: euphoric advance where heavy buying meets strong selling.
- Automatic Rally: rally after selling pressure temporarily exhausts.
- Automatic Reaction: decline after demand temporarily exhausts.
- Secondary Test: revisit of the climax area with reduced spread, volume, and continuation ability.
- Spring: temporary break below support followed by recovery into range.
- Upthrust: temporary break above resistance followed by rejection into range.
- UTAD: late upthrust after distribution, often the decisive test before markdown.
- SOS: bullish movement with wide spread, displacement, strong close, and supportive volume.
- SOW: bearish movement with wide spread, displacement, strong close lower, and supportive volume.
- LPS: reaction after strength that holds as a higher low.
- LPSY: rally after weakness that holds as a lower high.

## Distinguishing Accumulation From Distribution

### Evidence Favoring Accumulation

- Prior downtrend loses momentum.
- Selling climax is followed by strong rally.
- Secondary tests show reduced selling pressure.
- Volume decreases as the range develops.
- Downward waves make less progress.
- Springs recover quickly.
- Price fails to reach range lows.
- Bullish waves are stronger than bearish waves.
- Breakout above resistance shows effort/result harmony.
- Pullback after breakout holds.

### Evidence Favoring Distribution

- Prior uptrend loses momentum.
- Buying climax is followed by strong reaction.
- Secondary tests show reduced buying pressure.
- Volume spikes appear through the range.
- Upward waves make less progress.
- Upthrusts reject quickly.
- Price fails to reach range highs.
- Bearish waves are stronger than bullish waves.
- Breakdown below support shows effort/result harmony.
- Rally after breakdown fails.

The strongest evidence is usually the latest meaningful shakeout and the reaction after it.

## Structural Failure

Bullish structural failure: price should rotate to the lower boundary but turns up before reaching it. Sellers failed to complete the job.

Bearish structural failure: price should rotate to the upper boundary but turns down before reaching it. Buyers failed to complete the job.

Structural failure matters most after a shakeout. Spring plus failure to revisit lows is strong bullish evidence. UTAD plus failure to revisit highs is strong bearish evidence.

## Shortening of the Thrust

SOT appears when each new push in the trend direction travels less distance. In an uptrend it warns of deteriorating demand or appearing supply. In a downtrend it warns of deteriorating supply or appearing demand. It is a warning, not an entry signal.

## Sloping Structures

Schematics are training tools. The market repeats in logic, not identical shape.

- Upward-sloping accumulation: strong buyer control.
- Downward-sloping accumulation: possible if selling pressure deteriorates and buyers later break bearish structure.
- Downward-sloping distribution: strong seller control.
- Upward-sloping distribution: possible if upward progress shortens and market fails after upthrust/lower high.

## Volume Analysis

High volume can mean continuation, absorption, exhaustion, stop activation, or institutional activity. Low volume can mean valid test, lack of interest, absence of supply/demand, or poor breakout quality. Meaning depends on location and result.

Effort/result examples:

| Price Action | Volume | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Wide bullish spread, strong close | High | Demand in harmony |
| Wide bearish spread, weak close | High | Supply in harmony |
| Narrow candle after heavy volume | High | Absorption or exhaustion |
| Pullback after breakout | Low | Potential valid test |
| Breakout with low volume | Low | Possible false break |
| High volume at range low, no downside progress | High | Potential buyer absorption |
| High volume at range high, no upside progress | High | Potential seller absorption |

## Volume Profile and Value

Important references:

- VPOC: most traded price in selected profile
- VAH: value area high
- VAL: value area low
- HVN: accepted value node
- LVN: low-participation zone, often rejection or fast movement
- VWAP: dynamic value reference

Principles:

- Inside value, expect rotation between extremes.
- Reject outside value and re-enter: expect movement back through value.
- Break value and accept outside: expect continuation.
- Start reversion but fail: expect continuation opposite the failed move.

## Asset-Agnostic Evidence Stack

The same Wyckoff questions apply across equities, crypto, futures, FX, and commodities, but the evidence source changes by asset class. Always record the adapter in `asset_context` before scoring the trade posture.

Evidence hierarchy:

1. Price structure and volume: primary evidence. Never let a secondary indicator override clear structure.
2. Value profile: VPOC, VAH, VAL, HVN, LVN, or a clearly marked fallback. Missing value lowers confidence and usually blocks clean-trade language.
3. Anchored VWAP: campaign cost basis from meaningful anchors such as markdown high, terminal low, spring, UTAD, SOS/SOW, major gap, or breakout.
4. Relative strength: proof of sponsorship or weakness versus a benchmark set appropriate to the asset.
5. Weis Wave or manual wave table: effort/result across the latest 3-5 meaningful waves.
6. Momentum SOT: MACD/AO or price/spread divergence only as shortening-of-thrust warning evidence, never as an entry trigger.
7. Order flow: execution confirmation only after context, location, trigger, and invalidation are defined.

Adapter examples:

- Equities/ADRs/ETFs: exchange volume is useful; compare to `SPY`, `QQQ`, and sector ETFs; use earnings gaps and campaign high/low anchors.
- Crypto: sessions are 24/7; compare to `BTC`, `ETH`, `TOTAL`, `BTC.D`, and sector leaders; use CVD, open interest, funding, liquidation, and spot/perp divergence when available.
- Futures: separate RTH and ETH when needed; use session profile, VWAP, contract volume, CVD/footprint, and roll caveats.
- FX: spot volume is proxy evidence; prefer futures proxies, DXY/cross-relative strength, and only use profile/VWAP when the feed supports meaningful volume.

## Order Flow Confirmation

Use order flow only after context and location are clear.

Long sequence:

1. Higher timeframe bullish context.
2. Price reaches support, spring, LPS, VAL, LVN, or VWAP.
3. Selling absorption appears.
4. Initiative buying appears.
5. Enter on trigger or pullback.
6. Stop below invalidation.

Short sequence:

1. Higher timeframe bearish context.
2. Price reaches resistance, UTAD, LPSY, VAH, LVN, or VWAP.
3. Buying absorption appears.
4. Initiative selling appears.
5. Enter on trigger or pullback.
6. Stop above invalidation.

## Multi-Timeframe Alignment

Best long conditions:

- Higher timeframe is accumulation, reaccumulation, or markup.
- Price tests higher-timeframe support or value low.
- Lower timeframe shows spring, LPS, or bullish structural failure.

Best short conditions:

- Higher timeframe is distribution, redistribution, or markdown.
- Price tests higher-timeframe resistance or value high.
- Lower timeframe shows UTAD, LPSY, or bearish structural failure.

Avoid lower-timeframe longs directly into higher-timeframe supply unless the plan is a scalp. Avoid lower-timeframe shorts directly into higher-timeframe demand unless the plan is a scalp.

## Scenario Planning

A complete scenario has:

- Primary case
- Alternative case
- Invalidation
- Confirmation behavior
- Entry zone
- Stop location
- Target logic
- Management rules

## Chart Illustration Model

Wyckoff reports should make the reasoning visible. Prefer a small chart pack:

- Macro Context: major supply/demand, range extremes, Creek/Ice, swing liquidity, and value/profile levels.
- Wyckoff Structure: range, phases, confirmed events, waves, shortening of thrust, structural failures.
- Trade Posture: entry zone, trigger, invalidation, targets, and no-trade zone if price is mid-range.
- Execution Refinement: lower-timeframe trigger, retest/absorption zone, stop, and first liquidity only when needed.

Autofit rule:

- Fit or explicitly zoom the chart before drawing and screenshotting.
- The visible chart must include the whole structure being discussed.
- If prior range extremes, swing origin, breakout, or invalidation are off-screen, the chart is not acceptable evidence yet.
- Use separate fitted screenshots when macro context and execution detail cannot fit cleanly in one view.

Drawing rules:

- Use rectangles for zones.
- Use horizontal lines for levels.
- Use trend lines for wave evidence.
- Use text sparingly for decision labels.
- Do not label uncertain events as fact.
- Keep screenshots readable enough for both user review and downstream agent review.

Each screenshot should answer three questions:

1. What does this chart prove?
2. What would confirm the read?
3. What would invalidate the read?

## Entry Models

Phase C entry: aggressive near spring or UTAD. Best reward/risk, highest uncertainty.

Phase D entry: after SOS/SOW on LPS/LPSY. More confirmation, worse price.

Phase E entry: after breakout and confirmation. Highest structural confirmation, often lower reward/risk.

## Stops and Targets

Stops belong beyond invalidation, not at an emotionally comfortable distance.

Long stops:

- Below spring low
- Below LPS
- Below BUEC reaction low
- Below value reclaim level
- Below absorption zone

Short stops:

- Above UTAD high
- Above LPSY
- Above breakdown retest high
- Above failed VAH acceptance level
- Above absorption zone

Targets:

- Opposite side of range
- VPOC, VAH, VAL, HVN, LVN
- Prior swing highs/lows
- Measured move from range height
- Higher-timeframe supply/demand
- Trend channel extremes

Exit or reduce if breakout fails, continuation does not appear, opposite absorption appears, price rejects major value, or effort/result turns against the position.

## Common Mistakes

- Labeling instead of analyzing.
- Calling every range accumulation or distribution.
- Ignoring higher-timeframe context.
- Trading the middle of the range.
- Treating volume mechanically.
- Entering before confirmation.
- Refusing to change bias.
- Using order flow without context.
- Oversizing because the setup looks obvious.
- Studying only hindsight examples.
