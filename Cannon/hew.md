# The Definitive Guide to Harmonic Elliott Wave Analysis

## Purpose of This Guide

This guide is a practical operating manual for Harmonic Elliott Wave (HEW) analysis. It is written for analysts and traders who want a repeatable framework for mapping market structure, building alternate counts, projecting targets, and deciding when a setup is actionable versus when it is only structural noise.

HEW is not an automated signal system. It is a structural forecasting discipline. The analyst’s job is to identify the active fractal, validate it with ratios and invalidation rules, and then decide whether price is in a campaign zone, a confirmation zone, a terminal zone, or a no-trade zone.

The objective is not to label every wiggle. The objective is to identify the dominant count with enough evidence to make a clean decision.

---

## 1. What Harmonic Elliott Wave Is

Harmonic Elliott Wave is a ratio-driven evolution of Elliott Wave analysis. It keeps the broad fractal idea of market movement but changes the internal logic of impulsive waves.

Traditional Elliott Wave often treats impulses as five-wave structures where some waves extend, truncate, or form diagonals. HEW removes much of that ambiguity by using a more consistent internal structure:

* A complete trend sequence is still treated as a five-wave fractal.
* The directional waves inside that fractal are built from A-B-C motion.
* The internal action waves are validated by projection ratios, retracement ratios, alternation, and support/invalidation rules.
* The analyst works from the higher timeframe down into the lower timeframe, not from every small swing upward.

A simple way to think about HEW:

> Markets move in nested A-B-C structures that combine into five-wave fractals. Those five-wave fractals must obey structural rules, ratio clusters, and alternation.

HEW is strongest in liquid markets governed by mass psychology: major indices, liquid stocks, FX, major commodities, and major crypto pairs. It is weaker in illiquid markets, heavily manipulated instruments, tiny float stocks, and instruments where one actor can dominate price.

## 2. The Core HEW Fractal

The basic bullish HEW campaign is:

```text
Wave 1 up
Wave 2 down
Wave 3 up
Wave 4 down
Wave 5 up
```

Each motive leg can be decomposed into internal A-B-C structure:

```text
Wave 1 = A-B-C up
Wave 3 = A-B-C up
Wave 5 = A-B-C up
```

The bearish structure is the same logic inverted:

```text
Wave 1 down
Wave 2 up
Wave 3 down
Wave 4 up
Wave 5 down
```

The important conceptual shift is this: HEW does not treat a trend as a random collection of swing highs and lows. It treats a trend as a nested hierarchy of repeated A-B-C building blocks that must align across degree.

### Action, Reaction, Resolution

Every A-B-C structure can be read as:

* **A = Action**: the first directional move.
* **B = Reaction**: the counter-move or emotional test.
* **C = Resolution**: the final directional move that confirms or completes the pattern.

In a bullish A-B-C:

```text
A rises, B pulls back, C rises again.
```

In a bearish A-B-C:

```text
A falls, B rebounds, C falls again.
```

The C wave is not random. It directly relates to wave A through projection ratios.

## 3. HEW Versus Traditional Elliott Wave

HEW differs from traditional Elliott Wave in several important ways.

### 3.1 No Reliance on Extended Waves

Traditional Elliott Wave often explains strong trends through extended waves. HEW replaces that with a more consistent ratio framework. A strong Wave 3 is not called extended simply because it is large; it must meet specific projection criteria.

### 3.2 No Failed Fifths as a Core Feature

HEW expects Wave 5 to normally exceed the Wave 3 extreme. When it does not, the analyst should not immediately accept a failed fifth. The first task is to test whether the count is wrong, whether the alleged Wave 5 is actually part of a correction, or whether a higher-degree structure is being misread.

### 3.3 No Leading or Ending Diagonals as Primary Explanations

HEW generally absorbs many structures that traditional Elliott Wave might label as diagonals into its A-B-C internal structure. This reduces the habit of forcing a count to fit a visual wedge.

### 3.4 Ratio Clusters Matter More Than Visual Symmetry

A count that looks pretty but misses the ratio logic is weak. A count that looks noisy but obeys projection, retracement, support, alternation, and degree logic is stronger.

### 3.5 Top-Down Identification Is Preferred

Traditional counting often starts from the smallest visible swing and works upward. HEW is more efficient when the analyst first zooms out, finds the major markers, validates the likely five-wave fractal, and only then drills into lower-degree detail.

## 4. Notation and Degree Management

Notation must stay consistent. Use different label styles to separate wave degree.

A practical notation ladder:

```text
Highest degree: [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] / [A] [B] [C]
Large degree:   (1) (2) (3) (4) (5) / (A) (B) (C)
Medium degree:  1 2 3 4 5 / A B C
Lower degree:   (i) (ii) (iii) (iv) (v) / (a) (b) (c)
Micro degree:   i ii iii iv v / a b c
```

The exact notation is less important than consistency. Never mix degrees casually. If you label a structure as Wave 3, its internal A-B-C must be of one lower degree. If you label Wave C of 3, its internal motive structure must be one degree lower again.

### Degree Rule

A completed five-wave fractal creates a completed wave of one higher degree.

Example:

```text
A five-wave advance can complete Wave A of a larger A-B-C.
A five-wave decline can complete Wave A of a larger bearish correction.
```

This is why HEW analysis is fractal. Every label implies both a local structure and a higher-degree consequence.

## 5. The Core Rules of a Five-Wave HEW Fractal

These are the minimum structural rules.

### Rule 1 — Wave 2 Must Not Violate the Start of Wave 1

In a bullish count, Wave 2 cannot break below the starting low of Wave 1.

In a bearish count, Wave 2 cannot break above the starting high of Wave 1.

If this happens, the five-wave count is invalid.

### Rule 2 — Wave 3 Must Exceed the Wave 1 Extreme

In a bullish count, Wave 3 must trade above the Wave 1 high.

In a bearish count, Wave 3 must trade below the Wave 1 low.

A Wave 3 that cannot exceed Wave 1 is not a valid Wave 3.

### Rule 3 — Wave 3 Cannot Be the Shortest and Should Be Longer Than Wave 1

HEW goes further than classic Elliott logic: Wave 3 should not only avoid being the shortest; it should normally validate with a minimum projection from Wave 1.

The key validation floor is:

```text
Wave 3 minimum = 176.4% of Wave 1, projected from the end of Wave 2
```

A candidate Wave 3 that fails to reach the 176.4% floor is suspect and usually demands a recount.

### Rule 4 — Wave 4 Must Not Violate the Critical Support or Resistance Created by Wave B of 3

In a bullish count, Wave 4 should not break below the low of Wave B of 3.

In a bearish count, Wave 4 should not break above the high of Wave B of 3.

This is one of the most important HEW rules because it protects the internal integrity of Wave 3.

### Rule 5 — Wave 5 Normally Exceeds the Wave 3 Extreme

In a bullish count, Wave 5 normally makes a new high above Wave 3.

In a bearish count, Wave 5 normally makes a new low below Wave 3.

If price fails to do this, do not immediately force a terminal count. Recheck the entire structure.

### Rule 6 — Wave B of 5 Must Respect Wave 4

In a bullish count, Wave B of 5 should not violate the Wave 4 low.

In a bearish count, Wave B of 5 should not violate the Wave 4 high.

This keeps Wave 5 structurally attached to the completed Wave 4 correction.

## 6. The HEW Ratio Engine

Ratios are not decoration. They are the validation mechanism.

A count is weak until the ratio work supports it.

### 6.1 Projection Versus Retracement

A **projection** measures a completed directional wave and projects a future directional target.

A **retracement** measures how much of a prior move has been corrected.

Projection answers:

```text
Where should the next directional wave complete?
```

Retracement answers:

```text
How much of the prior move did the correction erase?
```

### 6.2 Basic Projection Formulas

Bullish Wave C target:

```text
Wave C target = Wave B low + (Wave A length × projection ratio)
```

Bearish Wave C target:

```text
Wave C target = Wave B high - (Wave A length × projection ratio)
```

Bullish Wave 3 target:

```text
Wave 3 target = Wave 2 low + (Wave 1 length × projection ratio)
```

Bearish Wave 3 target:

```text
Wave 3 target = Wave 2 high - (Wave 1 length × projection ratio)
```

Bullish Wave 5 target:

```text
Wave 5 target = Wave 4 low + ((Wave 1 + Wave 3 distance) × projection ratio)
```

Bearish Wave 5 target:

```text
Wave 5 target = Wave 4 high - ((Wave 1 + Wave 3 distance) × projection ratio)
```

### 6.3 Common Projection Ratios

#### Wave C Relative to Wave A

Common working ratios:

```text
100.0%
109.2%
114.4%
123.6%
138.2%
144.4%
176.4%
```

Use these to project the end of a simple A-B-C structure.

#### Wave 3 Relative to Wave 1

Core ratios:

```text
176.4%
200.0%
223.6%
276.4%
323.6%
```

The 176.4% level is the minimum validation floor in many HEW frameworks. The 200.0% and 223.6% levels are common stronger-wave objectives.

#### Wave 5 Relative to Waves 1 + 3

Common ratios:

```text
50.0%
61.8%
76.4%
85.4%
```

Wave 5 is often less explosive than Wave 3, but it can still stretch sharply when sentiment becomes euphoric or panic-driven.

### 6.4 Common Retracement Ratios

Frequently used retracement ratios include:

```text
23.6%
38.2%
41.4%
50.0%
58.6%
61.8%
66.7%
76.4%
85.4%
91.2%
98.2%
```

The context determines which ratios matter. A Wave 2 retracement behaves differently from a Wave 4 retracement. A Wave B inside an impulsive wave behaves differently from a Wave B inside a corrective structure.

## 7. The Standard Five-Wave HEW Model

The following is a practical working model for a bullish five-wave structure. Invert the logic for bearish structures.

### Wave 1

Wave 1 is the first A-B-C advance off a meaningful low.

It often emerges when sentiment is still damaged. Many traders dismiss it as a dead-cat bounce or bear-market rally.

The analyst’s job is not to believe the rally emotionally. The job is to mark the structure and wait for Wave 2 validation.

### Wave 2

Wave 2 is the first major test of the new trend.

It can be deep. It can scare bulls out. It can retrace 61.8%, 66.7%, 76.4%, 85.4%, or even more of Wave 1, but it must not break the Wave 1 origin.

Ideal Wave 2 behavior:

* corrects deeply enough to reset sentiment,
* holds above the Wave 1 start,
* forms a recognizable corrective structure,
* creates a clear invalidation level.

### Wave A of 3

Wave A of 3 is the first directional leg of the strongest part of the campaign.

It is often only slightly bigger than Wave 1. In many standard cases, analysts watch for ratios such as:

```text
Wave A of 3 ≈ 109.2% / 114.4% / 123.6% of Wave 1
```

### Wave B of 3

Wave B of 3 is a critical corrective test.

Common retracement references include:

```text
50.0%
61.8%
66.7%
76.4%
```

Wave B of 3 should not violate the Wave 2 low in a bullish structure. If it does, the count is damaged or invalid.

### Wave C of 3

Wave C of 3 is the heart of the trend.

This is usually the strongest, most emotional, most vertical part of the structure. It is the section where price can gap over prior resistance, fail to offer clean pullbacks, and force traders into emotional decisions.

Important requirements:

* Wave C of 3 should be stronger than Wave A of 3.
* Wave C of 3 should not be shorter than Wave A of 3.
* Wave C of 3 should often reach major projection targets from Wave A of 3.
* The whole Wave 3 should normally validate at or beyond 176.4% of Wave 1.

Common C-of-3 projection references:

```text
C of 3 = 123.6% of A of 3
C of 3 = 138.2% of A of 3
C of 3 = 176.4% of A of 3
C of 3 = 223.6% of A of 3 in stronger cases
```

### Wave 4

Wave 4 corrects the strongest part of the trend.

It often alternates with Wave 2. If Wave 2 was deep and simple, Wave 4 is more likely shallow and complex. If Wave 2 was shallow and brief, Wave 4 is more likely deeper and more complex.

Wave 4 must respect the critical low or high of Wave B of 3.

Common retracement references:

```text
23.6%
33.3%
38.2%
41.4%
50.0%
58.6%
61.8%
```

The strongest bullish counts often preserve Wave 4 above the B-of-3 support zone.

### Wave 5

Wave 5 is the final advance of the five-wave sequence.

In HEW it is commonly treated as an A-B-C structure. It should normally exceed the Wave 3 high in a bullish count. Its internal Wave B must respect the Wave 4 low.

Wave 5 can be deceptive. It can feel euphoric, but it may also be structurally weaker than Wave 3. The analyst should focus on target confluence and terminal behavior rather than emotional momentum.

## 8. Wave C of 3: The “Heart of the Rally”

Wave C of 3 deserves special attention because it is usually where the largest opportunity and the largest emotional error occur.

### Characteristics

In a bullish structure, Wave C of 3 often shows:

* acceleration through prior resistance,
* gaps or impulsive candles,
* limited pullbacks,
* rising slope versus Wave A of 3,
* emotional pressure on under-positioned bulls,
* forced covering by bears,
* vertical movement near the terminal section.

In a bearish structure, invert the behavior:

* acceleration through prior support,
* downside gaps,
* weak bounces,
* panic expansion,
* forced liquidation,
* vertical selloff into the terminal section.

### Validation

To validate a candidate Wave C of 3:

1. Measure Wave A of 3.
2. Project C-of-3 ratios from the end of Wave B of 3.
3. Confirm that Wave C of 3 is not shorter than Wave A of 3.
4. Confirm that the entire Wave 3 reaches at least 176.4% of Wave 1 from the Wave 2 low.
5. Compare slope: C of 3 should usually be stronger than A of 3.
6. Check whether the internal C of 3 subdivides cleanly into lower-degree structure.

### Trading Implication

The best entry is usually not in the middle of Wave C of 3. The best entry is normally built earlier: at Wave 2 completion, at B-of-3 support, or at a confirmed breakout after B-of-3 holds.

Once Wave C of 3 is obvious to everyone, the move may already be advanced.

## 9. Corrective Structures

Corrections are where most analysts lose discipline. HEW treats corrections as necessary sentiment reset mechanisms, not random noise.

Corrections develop in:

```text
Wave 2
Wave 4
Wave B
Wave X
```

All corrective structures can appear in most corrective positions, with one important warning: Wave 2 should not be treated as a triangle in the core HEW framework.

### 9.1 Simple Zigzag

Structure:

```text
A-B-C
```

A simple zigzag is a correction through price. It is direct and usually retraces a meaningful portion of the prior move.

In a bearish correction after a rally:

* Wave A falls,
* Wave B rebounds,
* Wave C falls again.

A and C are the action waves. They should show more directional force than B.

### 9.2 Double Zigzag

Structure:

```text
A-B-C - X - A-B-C
```

A double zigzag extends the corrective process when one A-B-C is not enough. Wave X connects the two corrective structures and can be messy.

Use double zigzag logic when:

* the first correction is not deep enough,
* sentiment has not reset,
* price continues to grind in corrective form,
* the second A-B-C resolves toward the expected retracement zone.

### 9.3 Triple Three

Structure:

```text
A-B-C - X - A-B-C - X - A-B-C
```

Triple threes are difficult because they can look like impulses to inexperienced analysts. The difference is validation.

A triple three usually fails to meet impulsive projection requirements and lacks proper alternation between the supposed Wave 2 and Wave 4.

Checklist:

* Does the move meet the Wave 3 projection requirement?
* Does it show alternation?
* Are the internal A-B-C moves proportional?
* Is it correcting time rather than launching a new trend?

If the answer is weak, it may be a triple three, not an impulse.

### 9.4 Regular Flat

A regular flat begins with a three-wave move against the prior trend, then retraces back near the prior extreme, then resolves back toward or slightly beyond the first corrective extreme.

Bullish-trend correction example:

```text
Wave A down
Wave B up near prior high
Wave C down near or slightly beyond Wave A low
```

Flats represent correction through time more than correction through price.

### 9.5 Expanded Flat

An expanded flat is similar to a regular flat, but Wave B exceeds the prior extreme before Wave C reverses.

Bullish-trend correction example:

```text
Wave A down
Wave B up to a new high
Wave C down below or near the Wave A low
```

Expanded flats are common because Wave B moves in the direction of the larger trend and can become emotionally powerful.

### 9.6 Running Flat

A running flat is a strong-trend correction where Wave B exceeds the prior extreme and Wave C fails to travel far enough to break Wave A decisively.

This implies the countertrend side is weak.

Use caution: running-flat labels are often abused. Do not use them just to protect a preferred count.

### 9.7 Triangle

A triangle is a five-leg corrective structure:

```text
a-b-c-d-e
```

Key requirements:

* It has five waves.
* Each wave is corrective.
* The structure usually contracts in range.
* Wave E can fake a breakdown or breakout before reversing.
* After completion, price often resumes in the direction of the larger trend.

Triangle warnings:

* Do not label a structure a triangle just because trendlines converge.
* The first leg must be a proper corrective leg.
* The final Wave E must be watched carefully; it can run stops before reversing.
* Triangles can morph into more complex corrections.

### 9.8 Double Three

Structure:

```text
W-X-Y
```

A double three combines two different corrective patterns connected by an X wave.

Example:

```text
Regular flat = W
Messy rally = X
Simple zigzag or triangle = Y
```

Double threes are corrections through time. They show up when the market needs to burn time, frustrate both sides, and reset sentiment without necessarily creating a deep retracement.

Common locations:

* Wave 4 after a strong Wave 3,
* Wave B after a completed five-wave move,
* prolonged sideways corrections after emotional rallies or selloffs.

## 10. The Special Role of Wave B

Wave B is the wild card of HEW.

It can be clean, ugly, shallow, deep, news-driven, emotional, or structurally deceptive. It often absorbs the uncertainty that the market needs before the next directional wave.

There are two broad types of Wave B behavior.

### 10.1 Wave B Inside an Impulsive Structure

Inside motive waves, Wave B often moves against the main trend.

Example inside bullish Wave 3:

```text
Wave A of 3 up
Wave B of 3 down
Wave C of 3 up
```

This B wave is countertrend. It is usually more predictable and should respect its critical support.

### 10.2 Wave B Inside a Corrective Structure

Inside a larger correction, Wave B can move in the same direction as the main trend.

Example after a bullish impulse:

```text
Wave A down
Wave B up
Wave C down
```

Here Wave B rises in the direction of the larger trend. That makes it stronger, more deceptive, and often capable of retesting or exceeding the prior high.

This is why expanded flats and running flats can fool traders.

### 10.3 Practical Rules for Wave B

* Never assume Wave B must be clean.
* Never assume Wave B must stop at a shallow retracement.
* Inside corrective structures, Wave B can retest or exceed the prior extreme.
* Inside impulsive structures, Wave B must respect the relevant invalidation level.
* If a B wave becomes too emotional, wait for confirmation rather than forcing a count.

## 11. Alternation

Alternation is one of the strongest HEW confirmation tools.

The basic rule:

```text
Wave 2 and Wave 4 tend to alternate in depth, complexity, and duration.
```

If Wave 2 is:

```text
deep, simple, fast
```

then Wave 4 is more likely:

```text
shallow, complex, slower
```

If Wave 2 is:

```text
shallow, simple, brief
```

then Wave 4 is more likely:

```text
deeper, more complex, more time-consuming
```

### Why Alternation Matters

Alternation prevents the analyst from forcing any two corrections into convenient swing highs and lows. It also helps separate real impulses from triple threes.

If a supposed impulse has no meaningful alternation between the alleged Wave 2 and Wave 4, the count is suspect.

### Compensation Through Wave B

Sometimes alternation is imperfect. In those cases, the market may compensate through a deeper or more complex Wave B inside Wave 3 or Wave 5.

This is another reason Wave B must be treated carefully.

## 12. The Top-Down HEW Workflow

The top-down process is the preferred workflow.

The analyst starts with the weekly or daily chart, identifies the major campaign, then drills into lower timeframes only after the larger structure is coherent.

### Step 1 — Zoom Out

Start with the highest useful timeframe.

For liquid crypto and indices, use:

```text
Monthly → Weekly → Daily → 4H → 1H
```

For active trading, still begin with the weekly/daily map before dropping into execution timeframes.

Ask:

* Is the market in a completed five-wave move?
* Is it in a correction after a completed five-wave move?
* Is it building Wave 3?
* Is it finishing Wave 5?
* Is it in a large B wave?

### Step 2 — Find the Three Major Markers

In a bullish five-wave fractal, look for:

**Marker 1:** the longest A-B-C move in the middle of the trend. This is often Wave 3.

**Marker 2:** the corrective A-B-C down and following A-B-C up that makes a new high after the middle trend. This helps identify Wave 4 and Wave 5.

**Marker 3:** the starting A-B-C up off the major low and the following corrective A-B-C down that makes a higher low. This helps identify Wave 1 and Wave 2.

Once all three markers are visible, the analyst can test the count.

### Step 3 — Confirm Wave 3

Measure Wave 1.

Project 176.4% of Wave 1 from the Wave 2 low.

A valid Wave 3 candidate should reach or exceed that projection.

If it does not, the count is weak.

### Step 4 — Confirm Wave 5

Wave 5 should:

* exceed the Wave 3 high in a bullish count,
* subdivide as A-B-C,
* respect the Wave 4 invalidation level,
* show target confluence with Wave 5 projection ratios.

### Step 5 — Cross-Check Critical Supports

For a bullish count:

```text
Wave 2 low must stay above Wave 1 origin.
Wave B of 3 low must stay above Wave 2 low.
Wave 4 low must stay above Wave B of 3 low.
Wave B of 5 low must stay above Wave 4 low.
```

For a bearish count, invert these into resistance checks.

### Step 6 — Confirm Wave 3 Internal Structure

Measure Wave A of 3 and Wave C of 3.

Wave C of 3 should not be shorter than Wave A of 3.

If C of 3 is weaker than A of 3, the alleged Wave 3 may be something else.

### Step 7 — Drill Down Only After the Macro Count Works

Once the macro count passes structural tests, drop to lower timeframes to inspect:

* A and C of 1,
* A and C of 3,
* A and C of 5,
* the internal structure of B waves,
* whether the lower-degree targets align with higher-degree targets.

Do not begin by micro-counting every candle. That creates false precision and wastes time.

## 13. Building a Projection Map

A projection map is not a trade plan. It is a structural roadmap.

A good projection map shows:

* the primary count,
* the active wave,
* the next target box,
* the flip level,
* the hard invalidation level,
* alternate count trigger,
* whether price is early, mid-structure, or terminal.

### Projection Map Sequence

1. Anchor the macro low or high.
2. Label the completed waves.
3. Identify the active wave.
4. Project the next directional target.
5. Mark the invalidation level.
6. Mark the level that upgrades or downgrades the count.
7. Separate structural targets from execution entries.

### Target Confluence

The best HEW targets are not single ratios. They are clusters.

A strong target zone may include:

* Wave 3 projection from Wave 1,
* Wave C of 3 projection from Wave A of 3,
* lower-degree Wave v projection,
* prior resistance or support,
* channel boundary,
* momentum divergence.

The more independent reasons a zone has, the more important it becomes.

## 14. Invalidation and Flip Levels

HEW analysis should always state what would prove the count wrong.

### Hard Invalidation

A hard invalidation level breaks a rule.

Examples:

* Bullish Wave 2 breaks below Wave 1 origin.
* Bullish Wave 4 breaks below Wave B of 3.
* Bearish Wave 2 breaks above Wave 1 origin.
* Bearish Wave 4 breaks above Wave B of 3.

When hard invalidation triggers, the count is not “under pressure.” It is invalid.

### Flip Level

A flip level does not necessarily invalidate the count, but it changes posture.

Examples:

* A breakout above resistance converts a corrective B-wave thesis into a bullish continuation candidate.
* A breakdown below B-of-3 support shifts from accumulation to stand-aside.
* A daily close above a Wave 3 floor upgrades a developing impulse.

A professional HEW report should always distinguish:

```text
Hard invalidation = the count is wrong.
Flip level = the posture changes.
```

## 15. Trade Setup Logic

HEW is not a signal system, but it can create high-quality trade locations.

### 15.1 Best Long Setup Locations

Strong long setups often appear at:

* Wave 2 completion above the Wave 1 origin,
* B-of-3 support after Wave A of 3,
* Wave 4 support above B-of-3,
* Wave B of 5 support above Wave 4,
* breakout from a completed corrective triangle in the direction of the main trend,
* retest of a broken resistance level after HEW structure confirms.

### 15.2 Best Short Setup Locations

Strong short setups often appear at:

* bearish Wave 2 completion below the Wave 1 origin,
* B-of-3 resistance after Wave A of 3 down,
* Wave 4 resistance below B-of-3,
* Wave B of 5 resistance below Wave 4,
* breakdown from a completed corrective triangle,
* failure at a Wave C target cluster.

### 15.3 No-Trade Conditions

Stand aside when:

* the macro count is not validated,
* price is in the middle of a messy B wave,
* the alternate count materially changes posture,
* price is between flip and invalidation levels with no edge,
* Wave C of 3 is already vertical and late,
* risk/reward is poor even if the count is right,
* target confluence is absent.

### 15.4 Structural Setup Versus Execution Setup

A structural setup says:

```text
This area matters.
```

An execution setup says:

```text
This is where the trade can be entered with defined risk.
```

Do not confuse them. HEW often identifies the right zone before price gives an executable trigger.

## 16. Risk Architecture

A trade map requires more than a count.

Minimum requirements:

* entry trigger,
* stop level,
* invalidation logic,
* target zone,
* risk/reward,
* position size,
* alternate-count response,
* time stop or structural stop.

### Risk Formula

For a long trade:

```text
Risk = (Entry price - Stop price) × position size
```

For a short trade:

```text
Risk = (Stop price - Entry price) × position size
```

### Reward Formula

For a long trade:

```text
Reward = (Target price - Entry price) × position size
```

For a short trade:

```text
Reward = (Entry price - Target price) × position size
```

### Position Sizing Principle

The wave count does not determine position size. The distance to invalidation and the account risk limit determine position size.

Strong count, wide stop, poor reward/risk = no trade or reduced size.

Average count, tight invalidation, strong reward/risk = possible trade if confirmed.

## 17. Chart Construction Standards

A clear HEW chart should read like a structural map, not a cluttered label dump.

### 17.1 Macro Structure Chart

Must show:

* full weekly or monthly campaign,
* primary count,
* regime header,
* flip level,
* hard invalidation,
* major target or demand zone,
* one dominant channel or diagonal if useful.

Avoid:

* cropping only the current correction,
* hiding the prior motive leg,
* overloading the chart with micro labels.

### 17.2 Projection Map

Must show:

* forward structural path,
* next target box,
* alternate trigger,
* invalidation,
* fib levels if they support the argument.

Must not become:

* a generic entry-stop-target card,
* a risk plan when no live trade exists.

### 17.3 Alternate Count Chart

Use when conviction is below roughly 75% or when Count #2 changes posture.

Must show:

* why the alternate is different,
* what level activates it,
* what level kills it,
* what it implies next.

Do not include alternates as token disclaimers. They must be actionable branches.

### 17.4 Wave Anatomy Chart

Use when internals matter.

Good uses:

* A-of-3 / B-of-3 / C-of-3 analysis,
* local corrective anatomy,
* compact fib relationships,

## 19. Common Analyst Mistakes

### Mistake 1 — Starting Too Low in Timeframe

Micro-counting first creates confusion. Start with the weekly/daily campaign map.

### Mistake 2 — Treating Every Swing as a Wave

Not every swing is structurally important. HEW is about meaningful fractal relationships, not every candle pivot.

### Mistake 3 — Forcing a Triangle

A converging range is not automatically a triangle. The wave anatomy must qualify.

### Mistake 4 — Ignoring Wave 3 Minimum Projection

A Wave 3 that does not reach its projection floor is not a valid high-conviction Wave 3.

### Mistake 5 — Calling Every Strong Move Wave 3

Strength alone is not enough. Wave 3 must be structurally located, ratio-validated, and internally coherent.

### Mistake 6 — Confusing B-Wave Strength with Trend Confirmation

A powerful B wave inside a correction can exceed the prior extreme and still reverse. This is why expanded flats trap traders.

### Mistake 7 — Ignoring Alternation

If Wave 2 and Wave 4 look too similar, the count may be wrong.

### Mistake 8 — Trading Structure Without Trigger

A valid structural zone is not automatically a trade. Execution requires confirmation and risk definition.

### Mistake 9 — Treating HEW as a Signal System

HEW gives a map. It does not remove uncertainty, risk, or the need for execution discipline.

### Mistake 10 — Hiding Uncertainty

Uncertainty should be expressed through flip levels and alternate-count triggers, not vague language.

## 21. Practical HEW Workflow From Blank Chart to Decision

### Stage 1 — Frame the Market

Open the weekly chart.

Mark:

* major high,
* major low,
* dominant trendline or channel if useful,
* obvious impulse or correction,
* major support/resistance zones.

Write one sentence:

```text
The market is in a bullish/bearish/corrective macro regime unless price breaks [level].
```

### Stage 2 — Identify the Candidate Count

Mark the most likely:

```text
Wave 1
Wave 2
Wave 3
Wave 4
Wave 5
```

Do not perfect it yet. Just create the candidate structure.

### Stage 3 — Validate Wave 3

Measure Wave 1.

Project:

```text
176.4%
200.0%
223.6%
```

If the candidate Wave 3 does not meet the 176.4% floor, return to Stage 2.

### Stage 4 — Validate Supports

Check:

```text
Wave 2 versus Wave 1 origin
B of 3 versus Wave 2
Wave 4 versus B of 3
B of 5 versus Wave 4
```

If any critical support fails, the count must be downgraded or rejected.

### Stage 5 — Validate Internal A-B-C Structures

For Waves 1, 3, and 5, identify:

```text
A
B
C
```

For Wave 3, specifically compare:

```text
C of 3 versus A of 3
```

C of 3 should not be weaker or shorter than A of 3 in a strong impulse.

### Stage 6 — Locate the Active Wave

Determine whether price is currently in:

* Wave 2 correction,
* Wave A of 3,
* Wave B of 3,
* Wave C of 3,
* Wave 4 correction,
* Wave 5 advance,
* post-five-wave correction.

This determines whether the market is early, middle, late, or finished.

### Stage 7 — Build the Primary and Alternate

Primary count:

```text
The count with the best ratio and structural validation.
```

Alternate count:

```text
The count that becomes primary if a specific level breaks or confirms.
```

Do not create alternates that cannot be activated by price.

### Stage 8 — Decide Posture

Use one of four posture labels:

```text
BULLISH
BEARISH
ACCUMULATION
STAND ASIDE
```

Do not use bullish language if price is still inside a bearish invalidation zone. Do not use trade language if there is no execution trigger.

### Stage 9 — Publish the Validation Log

Record measurements and conclusions.

Example:

```text
Wave 1 = 100 points
Wave 2 held above origin
Wave 3 176.4% floor = +176.4 points from Wave 2 low
Candidate Wave 3 exceeded floor
B of 3 held above Wave 2
Wave 4 held above B of 3
Count status: valid
```

2. Identify the campaign before the trade.

# Addendum: Ratio Models, Castaway Trading Models, and the Wave-B Invalidation Ladder

## A. The Three HEW Ratio Models

The three ratio models classify the strength and extension profile of a five-wave HEW fractal. They are not separate theories. They are three operating templates for measuring how far Wave 3 and Wave 5 should stretch once Wave 1 and Wave 2 are known.

Use them in this order:

1. Start with Model 1 as the default.
2. Upgrade to Model 2 only when Wave 3 is clearly extending beyond the standard model.
3. Upgrade to Model 3 only when the structure is super-extended, usually inside the strongest subdivision of Wave C of 3.

### Model 1 — Standard Five Waves

Model 1 is the default HEW five-wave structure. It is commonly found as a subdivision of Wave A of 3 or Wave C of 1. It is strong enough to validate an impulse, but not so strong that the entire structure becomes parabolic.

Operational signature:

* Wave A of 3 is only moderately larger than Wave 1.
* Wave C of 3 validates the main impulse but usually stays near the 176.4%–214.6% extension family of Wave 1.
* Wave 5 normally stretches beyond Wave 3 but remains proportionate.

| Wave B of 3 alternative | 61.8% / 66.7% / 76.4% × Wave A of 3    |

How to use it:

* If Wave 3 reaches the 176.4% floor and stalls near 200% or 214.6%, keep Model 1 primary.
* If Wave 5 projects near 214.4%–223.6% of Wave 1 and also aligns with 50%–66.7% of Waves 1 + 3, that becomes the main completion window.
* If price blasts through the Model 1 Wave 3 zone without terminal behavior, upgrade to Model 2.

### Model 2 — Extended Five Waves

Model 2 is the extended template. It is used when the five-wave fractal is clearly stronger than standard but not yet in super-extension. It can appear after a completed five-wave move when the market begins a powerful corrective or reversal leg.

Operational signature:

* Wave A of 3 is already extended versus Wave 1.
* Wave B of 3 can be shallow relative to A of 3 but large relative to Wave 1.
* Wave C of 3 drives toward the 223.6%–241.4% extension family of Wave 1.
* Wave 5 aims near 285.4% of Wave 1.

How to use it:

* Upgrade to Model 2 when Model 1 targets are exceeded without a proper reversal or consolidation.
* Model 2 is especially useful when Wave 3 has broad participation and the slope of C of 3 is materially stronger than A of 3.
* If Wave 3 exceeds 241.4% of Wave 1 and price remains vertical, prepare for Model 3.

### Model 3 — Super Extended Five Waves

Model 3 is the super-extended template. It is usually reserved for the most powerful internal subdivisions, especially inside Wave C of 3. This is the model for the true vertical phase.

Operational signature:

## B. Castaway Trader Models

Castaway trading logic separates structural analysis from execution. The macro count tells you whether long or short setups are allowed. The micro setup tells you where risk can be controlled. The reward model tells you how to manage the position after entry.

### Model 0 — Trading Business Model

This is the statistical foundation.

A trader is not trying to win every trade. The model requires:

* more winning trades than losing trades over a sample,
* average gain larger than average loss,
* repeatable setups in the direction of a higher-timeframe fractal,
* risk controlled by stops, not by hope.

The key principle:

> Micro setups cannot be traded in isolation. They must be traded in the direction of an unfolding higher-timeframe impulsive or trending fractal.

### Model 1 — Macro Long Setup: Wave 1 Up Followed by Wave 2 Down

Structure:

```text
A-B-C up off a Major Low = Wave 1
A-B-C down = Wave 2
```

Purpose:

This is the first major buy-on-weakness model after a potential major low.

Requirements:

* Price makes an A-B-C advance off a major low.
* Price then corrects in A-B-C form.
* The correction must hold the major low.
* The retracement is often deep; deep is acceptable as long as invalidation holds.

Execution logic:

* Aggressive entry: buy into the deep corrective retracement if the structure is clean and risk is definable.
* Conservative entry: wait for a breakout confirmation, but understand that the move can be partly exhausted by then.
* Invalidation: break below the major low / Wave 1 origin.

Best use:

* Early trend reversal.
* First deep pullback after a potential bottom.
* Building an initial campaign position before Wave 3 begins.

### Model 1.2 — Nested 1-2 / i-ii Setup Off a Major Low

Structure:

```text
Wave 1 up
Wave 2 down
then smaller-degree i up
ii down
```

Purpose:

This is the stronger version of Model 1 because the market begins to show nested impulsive behavior after the first Wave 2 holds.

Requirements:

* Macro Wave 1 and Wave 2 are already visible.
* A smaller-degree first push appears off the Wave 2 low.
* A smaller-degree correction follows and must hold above the prior critical support.

Execution logic:

* Use the smaller-degree ii low as the tactical support.
* Keep the major low as the campaign invalidation.
* A breakout through the smaller-degree wave i high confirms local momentum.

Best use:

* Entering early Wave A of 3.
* Adding after the first macro low has already been defended.
* Improving risk versus buying the larger breakout later.

### Model 2.2 — Micro Flat Setup: (a) Up Followed by Flat A-B-C of (b) Down

Structure:

```text
(a) up
then flat a-b-c down of (b)
```

Purpose:

This is the refined micro setup for buying weakness after the first push off a major low.

Requirements:

* A first push up appears from a major low.
* Price corrects in a flat or A-B-C structure.
* The corrective structure retraces deeply enough, often at least 76.4% of the first push.
* Critical support must hold.

Execution logic:

Two variants exist:

1. **Anticipatory entry:** enter near the deep retracement while support holds.
2. **Buy-stop confirmation:** place a buy stop above the top of wave (a) up.

Risk:

* Stop goes below the critical support / major low.
* The setup is invalidated if the correction breaks the level that must hold.

Best use:

* Micro entry inside a larger bullish count.
* Entering before a full breakout, while risk is still small.
* Capturing the transition from correction into the next directional leg.

### Model 3 — Triangle After First Push Off the Bottom

Structure:

```text
(a) up
then a-b-c-d-e triangle consolidation as (b)
```

Purpose:

This model captures a market that refuses to give a clean deep pullback after the first push off the low. Instead of a deep Wave 2-style retracement, price compresses sideways.

Requirements:

* First push up off a major low.
* Corrective triangle forms after that push.
* Triangle support must hold, except for possible brief fake breakdown behavior.
* The triangle should complete all five legs before the next rally.

Execution logic:

* Conservative entry: buy the triangle breakout.
* Aggressive entry: buy the final E-wave support hold if the triangle anatomy is clear.
* Invalidation: sustained break below triangle support or major low.

Best use:

* Strong markets where bulls do not allow a deep retracement.
* Early Wave 3 development.
* Continuation after compression.

### Model 4 — Breakout Confirmation Model

Purpose:

This is the conventional confirmation model.

Logic:

* Wait for price to break above the setup resistance.
* Enter after confirmation instead of buying weakness.

Problem:

The source framework warns that confirmation can come late. Price may need to make a large move before the breakout is obvious, and the move can already be exhausted by the time the trader enters.

Best use:

* When the count is unclear but a breakout would resolve it.
* When the trader prefers lower structural uncertainty over better reward/risk.
* When buying weakness is too risky because invalidation is unclear.

### Model 5 — Targeted Reward-Risk Management Model

The Castaway setup model uses risk multiples for targets.

Basic structure:

```text
Risk = entry to stop distance
Target 1 = 123.6% × risk
Target 2 = 223.6% × risk
Target 3 = 423.6% × risk
```

Management model:

* At Target 1: move stop to breakeven.
* At Target 2: sell 50% of the position.
* At Target 3: sell the remaining 50%.

Purpose:

This model creates a system where average winners can exceed average losers even if some trades only reach Target 1 or stop out after partial progress.

### Model 6 — Stand-Aside Model

Stand aside when:

* the higher-timeframe count is not impulsive or trending,
* the micro setup is not aligned with the macro direction,
* price is trapped between flip and invalidation levels,
* the correction violates the support that must hold,
* the reward/risk is poor after confirmation,
* the market is inside a messy B wave.

No trade is a valid trading model. In HEW, the analyst is paid to wait for structure, not to force action.

## C. The Wave-B Invalidation Ladder That Must Hold

The Wave-B ladder is the structural spine of the five-wave fractal. In a bullish count, each new higher low becomes the next critical support that the following correction must not violate. In a bearish count, each new lower high becomes the next critical resistance that the following correction must not violate.

### Bullish Wave-B Ladder

The bullish ladder works upward from the major low.

```text
Major Low / Wave 1 origin
↓ must hold for Wave 2
Wave 2 low
↓ must hold for Wave B of 3
Wave B of 3 low
↓ must hold for Wave 4
Wave 4 low
↓ must hold for Wave B of 5
Wave B of 5 low
↓ must hold while Wave 5 completes
```

### Bullish Rule Set

1. **Wave 2 must hold above the Wave 1 origin.**

   * If Wave 2 breaks the origin of Wave 1, the entire bullish five-wave count is invalid.

2. **Wave B of 3 must hold above the Wave 2 low.**

   * After Wave A of 3 completes, the correction in B of 3 must not violate Wave 2.
   * If B of 3 breaks Wave 2, the Wave 3 thesis is invalid.

3. **Wave 4 must hold above the Wave B of 3 low.**

   * B of 3 becomes the critical support for Wave 4.
   * If Wave 4 breaks B of 3, the five-wave bullish structure is invalid or must be materially reworked.

4. **Wave B of 5 must hold above the Wave 4 low.**

   * After Wave A of 5, the B-wave pullback must not break Wave 4.
   * If it breaks Wave 4, the Wave 5 thesis is invalid.

5. **The same rule repeats at lower degree.**

   * If you count smaller-degree (i) up and (ii) down, then smaller-degree (b) of (iii) must hold above (ii).
   * Every degree has its own ladder.

### Bearish Wave-B Ladder

Invert the bullish rules.

```text
Major High / Wave 1 origin
↑ must hold for Wave 2
Wave 2 high
↑ must hold for Wave B of 3
Wave B of 3 high
↑ must hold for Wave 4
Wave 4 high
↑ must hold for Wave B of 5
Wave B of 5 high
↑ must hold while Wave 5 completes
```

### Bearish Rule Set

1. Wave 2 must hold below the Wave 1 origin.
2. Wave B of 3 must hold below the Wave 2 high.
3. Wave 4 must hold below the Wave B of 3 high.
4. Wave B of 5 must hold below the Wave 4 high.
5. The same rule repeats at lower degree.

### What “Must Hold” Means

“Must hold” does not mean price can never wick through by a tick on a noisy lower timeframe. It means the structure cannot accept a decisive violation of the relevant level. The operating standard should be defined before analysis is published:

* intraday wick violation,
* candle close violation,
* daily close violation,
* weekly close violation,
* or full structural break.

For campaign maps, use daily or weekly closes unless the analysis is explicitly intraday.

### Practical Ladder Table

| Ladder Level          | Bullish Must Hold     | Bearish Must Hold     | Break Means                         |
| --------------------- | --------------------- | --------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Wave 2                | Above Wave 1 origin   | Below Wave 1 origin   | Count invalid                       |
| B of 3                | Above Wave 2          | Below Wave 2          | Wave 3 thesis invalid               |
| Wave 4                | Above B of 3          | Below B of 3          | Five-wave structure invalid/damaged |
| B of 5                | Above Wave 4          | Below Wave 4          | Wave 5 thesis invalid               |
| Lower-degree b of iii | Above lower-degree ii | Below lower-degree ii | Lower-degree impulse invalid        |

### Castaway Wording for Reports

Use decisive wording:

```text
Bulls must hold the B-of-3 low. A daily close below it invalidates the active Wave 4 thesis and forces a recount.
```

```text
This is not a buyable pullback unless Wave B of 3 holds above Wave 2. If that level fails, the structure is no longer an unfolding Wave 3.
```

```text
Wave B of 5 must hold above Wave 4. If it fails, the market is not building a valid final fifth wave; it is already correcting at a larger degree.
```
